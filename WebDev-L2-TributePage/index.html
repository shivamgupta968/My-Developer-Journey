01 / THE STORY

A life shaped by curiosity

From a small island town
to the heart of a nation.

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Raised in a modest family, he developed an early fascination with learning, flight and the world beyond the horizon. His determination carried him through his studies in physics and aeronautical engineering.

Kalam became one of India's most respected aerospace scientists. At ISRO, he led the project that developed the SLV-III, which placed the Rohini satellite into near-Earth orbit in 1980. Later, at DRDO, he helped guide important indigenous missile-development programmes.

In 2002, he became the 11th President of India. Known affectionately as the “People's President”, he brought warmth, humility and a deep belief in education to the office. He continued meeting students and encouraging them to think boldly about India's future.

Even after his presidency, teaching remained central to his life. On 27 July 2015, while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong, he passed away. His message lives on through the students, readers and dreamers he inspired.
